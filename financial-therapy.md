# Financial Therapy
*Individual therapy in Utah for adults navigating money, work, security, freedom, and what comes next*

You may be highly competent, thoughtful, and good with money. You planned carefully, postponed gratification, and built a level of financial security that has given you real options.

But having options is not always as freeing as you expected.

It can be frightening, isolating, and overwhelming, especially when the questions you’re facing don’t have spreadsheet answers.

*What is enough? What is money for? Should I keep pushing toward my number, change careers, step away from work, or build an entirely different kind of life?*

*What do I actually want?*

Often, something has shifted. You’ve reached a financial milestone, received a windfall, been laid off, watched your industry change beneath you, or realized you cannot keep doing what you’ve been doing.

The competence and planning that got you here have brought you somewhere valuable. But they cannot tell you where to go next.

These questions can be difficult to discuss with people who don’t understand your financial reality. Even within financial independence communities, the conversation often returns to calculations when the answers are deeply personal.

Here, you don’t have to translate yourself, minimize the choices you’ve created, or apologize for having them.

We can talk honestly about money, work, fear, freedom, identity, and what you want your life to be for.

------

## When the spreadsheet stops being enough

You may be nearing the financial independence number you’ve worked toward for years, and still find yourself saying, *One more year.*

Or perhaps the choice arrived before you were ready. You were laid off, burnout is affecting your performance, or the industry you built your career in is changing beneath you.

You may find yourself wondering:

- *Why can’t I grind it out a little longer?*
- *Who am I if I’m not working?*
- *Does changing course mean I failed?*
- *What if I can do something different but have no idea what I want?*

You may return to what you know: refining the assumptions, downloading another calculator, or running another Monte Carlo simulation.

The analysis may be sophisticated and financially sound. But some questions cannot be resolved by making the spreadsheet more precise.

------

## More options, less clarity

Financial independence is often imagined as a finish line. But financial security creates meaningful options long before full retirement.

You may already have room to take a sabbatical, reduce your hours, return to school, recover from burnout, care for family, or move into work that pays less but fits better.

Perhaps early retirement is exactly what you want. It may create space for relationships, travel, creativity, health, community, leisure, or meaningful unpaid work.

Or perhaps you might enjoy your job once you know staying is a choice. Maybe you want a different career rather than no career. You may want a life that moves between work, rest, family, and other forms of contribution.

The question may not be only:

*Can I retire?*

It may also be:

*What choices are already available to me?*
*What would I want my freedom to make possible?*
*What would I do on an ordinary Tuesday?*
*Where would I find structure, connection, purpose, and a sense of being useful?*

More options do not automatically create clarity. Sometimes they create paralysis.

You may also be navigating questions about relationships and belonging. One partner may be ready to leave work while the other is not. Retiring with children at home may mean stepping into a very different caregiving role. 

You may want a relationship, but worry about being taken advantage of. Or you may have spent years building independence and now find that commitment feels uncomfortably close to being trapped.

These are not failures of planning.

They are human questions about identity, security, purpose, connection, and how to use the freedom you worked so hard to create.

------

## What is enough?

Financial independence begins with an unconventional and imaginative idea: perhaps you do not have to follow the default script of working until traditional retirement age.

Reaching toward that freedom requires planning, competence, discipline, and the ability to postpone gratification. These are real strengths.

But strengths can become rigid when fear takes over.

Planning can become a search for certainty that does not exist.
Discipline can make changing course feel like failure.
Delayed gratification can make it difficult to recognize when the future has arrived.
Goal orientation can keep you moving toward a destination you no longer want.

You may be left asking:

- *What would actually make me feel secure?*
- *What am I afraid will happen if I stop accumulating?*
- *What does my work say about who I am?*
- *What would I lose if I changed course?*
- *What do I want my time, energy, and money to support?*

There may be several possible lives, each offering something meaningful and each involving real tradeoffs.

Financial therapy offers room to explore them without requiring a perfect answer.

## Money and trauma

Money can carry the emotional weight of earlier experiences.

You may have lived through poverty, instability, illness, divorce, betrayal, religious pressure, family conflict, job loss, or times when survival depended on being exceptionally responsible.

Even after circumstances change, your sense of safety may not change with them.

Financial success does not automatically resolve:

- fear of dependence
- shame about wanting or having money
- difficulty trusting other people
- fear of being exploited
- a need to remain prepared for catastrophe
- guilt about having more than people you care about
- experiences of financial control or betrayal
- traumatic memories connected to work, illness, caregiving, or loss

Therapy can help explore both the present-day question and what it touches from the past.

When appropriate, this work may include [**Accelerated Resolution Therapy (ART)**](https://acceleratedresolutiontherapy.com/what-is-art/) for difficult or traumatic experiences. [**Ketamine-Assisted Psychotherapy (KAP)**](https://chatgpt.com/kap/) may also support deeper work around identity, fear, grief, meaning, and transition.

The aim is not to dismiss realistic concerns. It is to understand more clearly how present circumstances and earlier experiences may both be shaping your choices.

------

## What financial therapy can offer

This work is not about finding one perfect life plan or eliminating uncertainty before you act.

Therapy can provide space to:

- put language to feelings that have been difficult to discuss
- explore what money, work, security, and success mean to you
- notice options you have dismissed or overlooked
- recognize when fear has narrowed your thinking
- make room for uncertainty, grief, contradiction, and competing values
- question the idea that there must be one risk-free choice
- construct small, realistic experiments
- reconnect with curiosity
- move toward meaningful choices despite fear

Sometimes the possibilities are large: leaving a career, retiring, returning to school, or rebuilding after a layoff.

Sometimes the next experiment is smaller: taking time away from work, volunteering, testing a new weekly structure, or beginning a conversation you have avoided.

Experiments allow you to learn from your actual life instead of trying to predict every possible future from inside your head.

------

## Room for the big questions

Career and financial coaching can be valuable when you need strategies, clearer goals, or a concrete plan.

Financial therapy offers something different.

There is room here to stay with questions that do not have immediate, solution-focused answers:

- *What makes my life meaningful?*
- *What do I want to contribute?*
- *Where do I feel seen and valued?*
- *What relationships and commitments matter to me?*
- *Where do I want stability, and where do I want growth?*

We can explore practical possibilities without using action steps to rush past emotional complexity.

The goal is not to prescribe a conventional life—or an unconventional one. It is to help you understand the choices you have and what you want those choices to support.

## What this work is—and isn’t

I provide **individual therapy** for adults. Relationship, family, and caregiving dynamics are welcome, but I do not provide couples therapy or financial mediation.

Financial therapy with me is not:

- financial planning or investment advice
- tax, legal, or retirement-distribution advice
- budgeting or debt-management coaching
- résumé writing or job-search consulting
- wealth management
- a substitute for a financial planner, accountant, attorney, or other qualified professional

I am not here to tell you whether you can afford to retire, how to invest, or which career will produce the best financial outcome.

I am here to help you explore the emotional, existential, and identity questions that remain after the projections have been run—and to help you use the information and options you already have with greater clarity and agency.
